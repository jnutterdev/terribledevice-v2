Navigating the neon-lit alleys of pop culture — one film, record, and game at a time.

terribledevice is a pop culture blog anchored in cyberpunk and sci-fi. It covers film, music, and games — mostly from the 1980s onwards, mostly at the stranger end of the spectrum.

Part criticism, part guided tour. Written for the curious as much as the already-converted. If you've never seen the film or heard the record, that's fine. That's kind of the point.

What Is This
§ 01
Sharp, sardonic, occasionally very silly. Enthusiastic without being breathless. Points at things and says you should look at this — and then explains why, whether you've heard of it or not.

The site started as a place to write about the stuff that doesn't fit neatly anywhere else — the B-movies that got there first, the electronic records that aged better than they should have, the games that used their medium to do something no other medium could.

Cyberpunk and sci-fi are the gravity well, but the orbit is wide. RoboCop, Total Recall, Fortress, Split Second — films that understood something about the future by not trying too hard to look like it. Autechre and Richard Devine making music that sounds like it came from inside a machine that's dreaming. Games that treat failure as a narrative tool rather than an obstacle.

The writing is mostly short — one idea, made well, and then out. Occasionally something demands more space, and those pieces get it. But the default is: say the thing, say why it matters, get out of the way.

Nothing here assumes prior knowledge. If you haven't seen Akira, we'll tell you why you should before we tell you what to think about it. The goal is to make the neon-lit alley feel navigable, not to stand at the entrance and check credentials.

The Pillars
§ 02
Screen

Film, television, anime — anything that moves. Heavy on cyberpunk and sci-fi but not limited to it. Interested in the films that predicted something, the ones that got it wrong in interesting ways, and the ones nobody's written about properly yet.

  • Blade Runner / 2049
  • Akira, Ghost in the Shell
  • RoboCop, Total Recall
  • Johnny Mnemonic, Nemesis
  • Strange Days, Split Second
  • The Matrix, Dark City
Sound

Music at the intersection of machine, texture, and future. Anchored in IDM and glitch, with threads into post-industrial, ambient, and a lane for synthwave that earns its place. Interested in records as habitats — environments you can live inside for forty minutes.

  • Autechre, Richard Devine, Gridlock
  • Farmers Manual, Fennesz, Dryft
  • Access to Arasaka, Subheim, Huron
  • Haujobb, Forma Tadre
  • Perturbator, Mega Drive, Mitch Murder
  • Vangelis, The Prodigy, Chemical Brothers
Interactive

Games and interactive fiction as cultural objects. Interested in what games can do that nothing else can — especially when they use the medium against itself. The failure state as narrative. The city as system. The interface as argument.

  • System Shock, Deus Ex
  • Disco Elysium, Planescape: Torment
  • Control, Remedy's connected universe
  • Cyberpunk 2077 (warts and all)
  • Beneath a Steel Sky
  • Syndicate, Uplink
Who

Editor / Writer

Writes about the places where technology, culture, and anxiety overlap. Has strong opinions about Vangelis, the second act of RoboCop, and whether Disco Elysium is the best novel of the 2010s. Probably has seen Akira too many times. Based somewhere with bad weather and good record shops.
